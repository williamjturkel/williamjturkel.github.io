# More Readings for a Field in Digital History

_February 1, 2007_  
Originally published at `https://digitalhistoryhacks.blogspot.com/2007/02/more-readings-for-field-in-digital.html`

Last month I posted a draft reading list[^1] for a general / comprehensive graduate exam in digital history. The list raised a fair amount of interest, and a number of people were kind enough to e-mail me suggested additions or blog about them. So, more or less in the order that I received them, here are more readings for a field in digital history. As before, I've included links to Amazon records to support further hacking. Suggestions are still welcome, of course.  

John Jordan[^2] emphasized the historical precursors of digital history, particularly systems theory.  

* Ackoff, Russell and Fred Emery. On Purposeful Systems: An Interdisciplinary Analysis of Individual and Social Behavior as a System of Purposeful Events[^3], new ed. Aldine, 2005.
* Bowker, Geoffrey C., Susan Leigh Star, William Turner and Les Gasser, eds. Social Science, Technical Systems, and Cooperative Work: Beyond the Great Divide[^4]. Lawrence Erlbaum Associates, 1997.
* Churchman, C. West. The Systems Approach[^5], rev. ed. Dell, 1984.
* Copeland, B. Jack, ed. The Essential Turing[^6]. Oxford, 2004.
* Emery, Fred E., ed. Systems Thinking[^7]. Penguin, 1981.
* Emery, Fred E. and Eric L. Trist. Towards a Social Ecology[^8]. Springer, 1975.
* Hodges, Andrew. Alan Turing: The Enigma[^9]. Walker and Company, 2000.
* Rogers, Everett M. Diffusion of Innovations[^10], 5th ed. Free Press, 2003.
* Russell, Stuart J. and Peter Norvig. Artificial Intelligence: A Modern Approach[^11], 2nd ed. Prentice Hall, 2002.
* Simon, Herbert A. Sciences of the Artificial[^12], 3rd ed. MIT, 1996.
* Simon, Herbert A. Administrative Behavior[^13], 4th ed. Free Press, 1997.
* Tufte, Edward R. The Visual Display of Quantitative Information[^14], 2nd ed. Graphics Press, 2001.

David Parry[^15] suggested a few theoretical works:  

* Bogost, Ian. Unit Operations: An Approach to Videogame Criticism[^16]. MIT, 2006.
* Derrida, Jacques. Archive Fever: A Freudian Impression[^17]. Chicago, 1998.
* Derrida, Jacques. Paper Machine[^18]. Stanford, 2005.
* Hayles, N. Katherine. My Mother Was a Computer: Digital Subjects and Literary Texts[^19]. Chicago, 2005.
* Heidegger, Martin. The Question Concerning Technology, and Other Essays[^20]. Harper, 1982.

Sheila Brennan[^21] sent two readings from her own list...  

* Burton, Orville Vernon, ed. Computing in the Social Sciences and Humanities[^22]. Illinois, 2002.
* Landow, George P. Hypertext 3.0: Critical Theory and New Media in an Era of Globalization[^23]. Johns Hopkins, 2006.

Jeremy Boggs[^24] posted a nice piece about the importance of design in digital history, which included the books listed below. (He also quoted Josh Greenberg[^25] to the effect that "You wouldn’t go to an academic conference to present a paper wearing dirty, torn jeans and ratty t-shirt." To which I'd have to reply that I've given more than my share of conference presentations in a black T-shirt and ratty cargo shorts. How the heck else could I perform my nerdiness? It's not like I wear horn rims or a pocket protector.)  

* Bickner, Carrie. Web Design on a Shoestring[^26]. New Riders, 2003.
* Clarke, Andy. Transcending CSS: The Fine Art of Web Design[^27]. New Riders, 2006.
* Curtis, Hillman. MTIV: Process, Inspiration, and Practice for the New Media Designer[^28]. New Riders, 2002.
* Krug, Steve. Don't Make Me Think: A Commonsense Approach to Web Usability[^29], 2nd ed. New Riders, 2005.
* Lenker, John. Train of Thoughts: Designing the Effective Web Experience[^30]. New Riders, 2002.
* Lupton, Ellen. Thinking with Type: A Critical Guide for Designers, Writers, Editors and Students[^31]. Princeton Architectural Press, 2004.
* McCloud, Scott. Understanding Comics: The Invisible Art[^32]. Harper, 1994. [added by Paula Petrik]
* McCloud, Scott. Reinventing Comics: How Imagination and Technology are Revolutionizing an Art Form[^33]. Harper, 2000. [added by Josh]
* Tufte, Edward R. Beautiful Evidence[^34]. Graphics Press, 2006.
* Wroblewski, Luke. Site-Seeing: A Visual Approach to Web Usability[^35]. Wiley, 2002.
* Zeldman, Jeffrey. Designing with Web Standards[^36], 2nd ed. Peachpit, 2006.

And last but not least, Tom Elliot[^37] pointed out Geoffrey Rockwell's great list of online readings in humanities computing[^38].
